# Beaver Valley: a game idea, and I want your honest feedback

## Page 1. The pitch

**Beaver Valley** is a working title. The real name is not chosen. The game
does not exist in finished form yet: what you have been given is an idea and
some decisions, and the art is placeholder.

### What it is

A cozy farming game that you play in a web browser or on a tablet. You inherit
a run-down farm, grow crops, forage in the wild, sell what you make, and slowly
rebuild the village hall with the village's help. It is in the spirit of Stardew
Valley and similar games, but with its own world, characters and story.

### Who it is for

People who like relaxed games with no pressure. There are no timers that end the
game, no game over, and a bad day costs you very little. It is a free hobby
project made by one person, so it will stay small at first and grow in layers.

### The feel we are aiming for

- **Cozy and unhurried.** A good evening is a few small decisions, some
  progress, a little curiosity, and a smile.
- **Funny and warm.** Affectionate jokes about everyday life and eccentric
  neighbours. Never grim.
- **A world with a past.** Old roads, ruins and a quiet shadow in the distance,
  but the shadow is backdrop, not a threat to you.
- **Your own pace.** Nothing tells you what to do. A neighbour nudges you
  toward the village hall. That is all.

### What matters most, in this order

1. **Farming and crafting.** The core of every day.
2. **Expeditions.** Day trips to wilder places, bringing goods home to the farm.
3. **Relationships.** Villagers you get to know, who matter from your first
   week and grow closer over time.

### How to read this packet

Pages 2 and 3 describe the setting and how the game plays. Pages 4 to 6 are the
feedback sheets. Please read pages 1 to 3 once, on your own, then fill in the
sheets before we talk. There are no right answers. "This bored me" and "I did
not get this" are the most useful things you can write.

## Page 2. The setting

### The premise

You inherit a neglected farm from a relative who has vanished down the road. They
were a quiet, shrewd gardener who loved rare seeds and went off adventuring to
find more. They left a trunk of odd seeds and one plain note: *"The soil is good,
the neighbours are better, do come and meet them."* Your family name is
Thimblemoss.

### The valley and its people

The valley is warm and quiet. Its people are the **Brimmers**: small, with long
ears that poke out around elaborate hats. They have a hat for every occasion and
trade. They keep **favour ledgers**, where neighbours write down favours given
and owed, and they are hospitable to a fault. They do these things out of
habit and no longer remember why.

One idea we like and want to test: a Brimmer's hat shows their standing in the
ledger. A newcomer wears a plain cap, a respected host wears something grand,
and your own hat grows as you do favours.

### The past

Long ago a kingdom stood along the old road. A dark lord broke it and was in turn
driven back and banished far away. The valley was the kingdom's kitchen garden,
and it survived because it was too small and dull to be worth his trouble. Its
folk are descended from the kingdom's servants.

The dark lord is not in the game as a character you meet. He is a **Hollow Crown**,
an empty crown and robes that rule without a body, with shadow and a great
voice mixed in. (The name and idea are a draft.) His influence can leak back in
as strange creatures at the far edges of the map, but never in the home valley.

### The town and the hall

Down the road is **Hearthwell**, a crossroads town where all kinds of travellers
pass through: tall road-folk, stout craftspeople, fey wanderers. Its heart is
**The Long Table**, the village hall, once an old waystation (an inn and a
granary) where travellers rested, ate and were seen on their way. Restoring it
is the first big goal. The town also has a general store, a smithy, an inn and
kitchen, a healer, a library and a festival green.

A group called the **Dawn Carriers** work as couriers, carrying post and parcels
between towns. Cheerful on the surface, they are secretly wardens who still watch
the road. Later, one breaks cover to tell you the road is not as safe as it was.

### Other peoples and festivals

Besides Brimmers, there is a forgetful wizard-like hermit in the woods who knew
your relative and is the nearest thing to an answer about where they went, dwarves in
the mountains, and elves. The first festival of the year is a **hat parade**,
where everyone shows off their best hat and their standing in the ledger.

### The ending

There is no real ending. When the last bundle for the village hall is finished
there is a village feast and a short scene, and then you keep playing.

## Page 3. How it plays

### A day

Each day runs from morning to the small hours. You have limited energy, so a
day is a set of choices: water the crops, forage, sell, visit someone, or go
to bed early. Running out of energy means you pass out and wake at home, with
a small cost. Nothing gets worse just because time passes. Seasons turn, and
that is all.

### Farming and foraging

You plant, water and harvest crops. Each crop has a season, a growth time and
a price. Wild goods grow near the farm and change with the season. Doing
things levels up your farming and foraging skills, which improves the quality
of your goods and lets you choose a specialty at level 5 (for example, crops
that sell for more, or crops that grow faster). You can upgrade your tools in
the shop. Weather matters: rain waters your plots, and you can check
tomorrow's forecast before you sleep.

### The village hall bundles

This is the main goal. The village hall asks for sets of goods: for example,
"any 4 of these 6 spring wild plants" or "three kinds of summer crop". You hand
items in one at a time, the hall remembers its progress, and finishing a bundle
gives a reward such as seeds or money. The first version has five bundles. Some
goods only exist in one season, so over time both farming and foraging matter.

### The world widens

The world is a set of rings around your home valley.

- **Ring 1, the home valley.** Farm, village and hall. Open from the start, safe
  and cozy.
- **Ring 2, the regions.** Four or five different places (think woods, marsh,
  mountain), each with its own goods and people. This opens partway through the
  hall chain, when one bundle mends the old bridge. Each region then needs its own
  tool or bridge to reach. This is where the shadow first shows up.
- **Ring 3, the dark edge.** Optional and late, behind a second, larger
  community project. Not designed yet.

Expeditions are day trips with limited energy and pack space. What you bring back
feeds your farm and the next community project.

### People

Every villager has a friendship value. You talk to them daily, give gifts (they
love some, like some, and dislike some), watch a heart meter fill, and see
small events. Friendship earns recipes, discounts and requests. This matters
from your first week. Over many updates we hope to reach a cast about as big as
Stardew Valley's, but that will be built up in layers.

### What is built so far

A playable first version exists in the browser with placeholder art: the day
cycle, farming, foraging, skills, a shop, the hall with five bundles, saving, and
a title and end screen. Villagers, the wider world and the story are still
only designs.
